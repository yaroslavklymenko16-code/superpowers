How this works

Your kid already loves Roblox. Creator Mode flips them from playing games to making them. Every mission builds one real piece of a game in Roblox Studio, with Claude as a coding helper, and teaches one big idea about how AI works.

The co-pilot rule (important)

Claude's terms require users to be 18 or older. So Creator Mode is built for you and your kid together: Claude runs on your account, on your screen, with you in the room. Your kid decides what to build and what to ask. You sit in the co-pilot seat. Many families find this is the best part.

What you need
  • A Windows PC or Mac (Roblox Studio doesn't run on phones, tablets or Chromebooks)
  • Roblox Studio, free from create.roblox.com
  • A Roblox account for your kid (turn on Parental Controls for under-13s)
  • A Claude account for you, free at claude.ai
What your kid learns
  • Real coding in Luau, Roblox's language
  • How AI tools predict text and why they make mistakes
  • How to give clear instructions (prompting)
  • How to test and fix code, and when not to trust AI
  • Online safety with AI

The Creator Hours deal (optional)

Want to rebalance screen time? Try this: hours spent building count differently from hours spent playing. Agree on a ratio together, like "one hour of creating earns thirty minutes of playing," and track it on the chart at the back of this book.

Setup · 20 min

Open the studio

Build
  1. Install Roblox Studio from create.roblox.com and log in with your Roblox account.
  2. Click New and pick the Baseplate template.
  3. Open these panels from the View tab: Explorer, Properties and Output. Output is where errors show up. Keep it open always.
  4. In a browser, open claude.ai on the grown-up's account. Put it next to Studio.
  5. Click Play (or press F5). Walk around. Press Stop to go back to building.
AI idea · Meet Claude

Claude is an AI that has read a huge amount of writing, including lots of code. When you type a message, it writes a reply one small piece at a time, picking what is most likely to come next. That's why it's good at code: code follows patterns. It's also why it can be confidently wrong. It doesn't "know" your game. It only knows what you tell it.

Hi Claude! I'm 11 and I'm learning to make games in Roblox Studio. I'm working with my parent. When you give me code, please explain it simply and tell me exactly where to put it in Roblox Studio.

Start every session with a message like this. It's called giving context: you'll learn why it matters in Mission 2.

Talk about it

Ask your kid: "Is Claude a person? How is it different from a friend?" (Good answer: it's a tool that predicts words. It's helpful, but it doesn't have feelings or remember you like a friend does.)

1
Your first script

The rainbow block

AI idea · Instructions in, results out

A computer program is a list of instructions. A script tells your game what to do. AI can write these instructions for you, but you are the game designer. You decide what should happen.

Build
  1. On the Home tab click Part. A grey block appears.
  2. In Explorer, hover over the part (it's inside Workspace), click +, and choose Script.
  3. Delete the line already in the script.
Write a Roblox Luau script that goes inside a Part and makes it change to a random color every second, forever.

Claude should give you something like this. Copy it into your script:

local part = script.Parent

while true do
	part.BrickColor = BrickColor.Random()
	task.wait(1)
end
Test it

Press Play. Does the block flash colors? If not, look in Output for red text and paste it to Claude.

Read the code

script.Parent means "the thing this script is inside." while true do means "repeat forever." task.wait(1) means "pause 1 second."

Challenge

Change the number in task.wait to make it faster. Then ask Claude to make the block also spin.

2
Prompting

The floor is lava

AI idea · Clear instructions get better answers

AI can only work with what you tell it. "Make lava" is vague. Is lava a floor? Does it hurt or kill? Does it glow? A good prompt says what you want, where it goes and how it should behave. This skill is called prompting.

make lava
Write a Roblox Luau script that goes inside a Part. When a player touches the part, their character should die and respawn. Explain each line.

Try both prompts. Which answer is more useful? Why?

Build
  1. Add a new Part. Make it flat and wide using the Scale tool.
  2. In Properties: set BrickColor to orange, Material to Neon, and tick Anchored so it doesn't fall.
  3. Rename it Lava (double-click its name in Explorer). Add a Script inside it.
local lava = script.Parent

lava.Touched:Connect(function(hit)
	local humanoid = hit.Parent:FindFirstChildOfClass("Humanoid")
	if humanoid then
		humanoid.Health = 0
	end
end)
Test it

Press Play and walk onto the lava. You should respawn. Touched fires when something bumps the part. Humanoid is the part of a character that has health.

Challenge

Write your own prompt to make lava that only takes away 20 health instead of all of it. You have to describe it clearly enough that Claude gets it right the first time.

3
Debugging

Coin collector

AI idea · AI makes mistakes. You're the boss.

AI writes code that looks right but sometimes isn't. It might guess a name wrong or forget a step. Real programmers always test. When something breaks, you become a detective: read the error, describe the problem, and ask for a fix.

Build part 1 · the scoreboard. In Explorer, add a Script inside ServerScriptService. It puts a "Coins" number on the leaderboard for every player.

local Players = game:GetService("Players")

Players.PlayerAdded:Connect(function(player)
	local leaderstats = Instance.new("Folder")
	leaderstats.Name = "leaderstats"
	leaderstats.Parent = player

	local coins = Instance.new("IntValue")
	coins.Name = "Coins"
	coins.Value = 0
	coins.Parent = leaderstats
end)

Build part 2 · a coin (with a bug!). Add a small yellow Part, tick Anchored, untick CanCollide, and put this script inside. It has a bug on purpose.

local coin = script.Parent
local Players = game:GetService("Players")

coin.Touched:Connect(function(hit)
	local player = Players:GetPlayerFromCharacter(hit.Parent)
	if player then
		player.leaderstats.Coin.Value += 1
		coin:Destroy()
	end
end)
Detective time

Press Play and touch the coin. The score doesn't go up! Read the red error in Output, then paste the error and the code to Claude.

My coin script has an error. Error from Output: [paste]. My script: [paste]. My leaderstats value is called "Coins". What's wrong?

Answer: the script says Coin but the scoreboard value is named Coins. One letter! Names must match exactly.

Challenge

Press Ctrl+D to copy the coin. Make 10 around your map. Then ask Claude how to make a coin spin.

4
Iteration

Moving platforms

AI idea · Build, test, improve, repeat

Nobody gets it perfect the first time, not game studios and not AI. The best way to work with AI is in small steps: ask for one thing, test it, then ask for the next change. Big messy requests give big messy code.

Build
  1. Add a Part, make it platform-shaped, and tick Anchored.
  2. Add this script inside. It slides the platform 20 studs back and forth forever.
local TweenService = game:GetService("TweenService")
local platform = script.Parent

local info = TweenInfo.new(
	3,                         -- seconds for one trip
	Enum.EasingStyle.Sine,     -- smooth start and stop
	Enum.EasingDirection.InOut,
	-1,                        -- repeat forever
	true                       -- go back the other way
)
local goal = { Position = platform.Position + Vector3.new(0, 0, 20) }

TweenService:Create(platform, info, goal):Play()
Test it, then change ONE thing
  1. Make it slower: change 3 to 6. Test.
  2. Make it go up instead of sideways: change Vector3.new(0, 0, 20) to Vector3.new(0, 15, 0). Test.
  3. Ask Claude for one more change. Test again.

The -- lines are comments. The computer ignores them; they're notes for humans. Ask Claude to add comments whenever code is confusing.

Challenge

Build a jumping course over your lava from Mission 2 using three moving platforms with different speeds.

5
How AI learns

Speed pads

AI idea · Patterns from examples

How did Claude learn to code? It was trained on a gigantic number of examples and learned the patterns: what usually comes after what. Try this game to feel it:

Predict the next word: "Once upon a ___." "Press F5 to ___." You guessed because you've seen those patterns before. AI does this, but with billions of examples. It finds patterns; it doesn't understand the world the way you do.

Explain to an 11-year-old how you learned to write code. Keep it short and be honest about what you can't do.
Build

Add a bright green, flat, anchored Part. Put this script inside. Step on it for a 3-second speed boost.

local pad = script.Parent
local boosted = {}

pad.Touched:Connect(function(hit)
	local humanoid = hit.Parent:FindFirstChildOfClass("Humanoid")
	if humanoid and not boosted[humanoid] then
		boosted[humanoid] = true
		humanoid.WalkSpeed = 50   -- normal speed is 16
		task.wait(3)
		humanoid.WalkSpeed = 16
		boosted[humanoid] = nil
	end
end)
Test it

Why do we need boosted? Delete those lines, test, and see what happens. (Touched fires many times per step. Without boosted, the boost keeps restarting.) This trick is called a debounce.

Challenge

Make a jump pad. Hint for your prompt: a Humanoid has a property called JumpPower, and you may need to turn on UseJumpPower.

6
AI safety

The secret door

AI idea · Smart and safe with AI
  • Keep private things private. Never type your full name, address, school, passwords or photos into an AI or a game chat.
  • Check facts. AI can make things up and sound sure. Test code; double-check facts with a grown-up or a trusted site.
  • AI is a tool, not a friend. It can be fun to talk to, but real friends and family are people.
  • You did the work. When you share your game, be proud, and be honest that AI helped.
Build

Add a tall, thin, anchored Part as a door. Put this script inside. Walk up and press E to open it.

local door = script.Parent

local prompt = Instance.new("ProximityPrompt")
prompt.ActionText = "Open"
prompt.ObjectText = "Secret Door"
prompt.Parent = door

local open = false

prompt.Triggered:Connect(function()
	open = not open
	door.Transparency = open and 0.7 or 0
	door.CanCollide = not open
	prompt.ActionText = open and "Close" or "Open"
end)
Test it

Can you walk through when it's open? Can you close it again? not open flips true to false and false to true.

Talk about it

Ask: "If someone in a game asked where you go to school, what would you do?" and "When might an AI be wrong?" Let your kid teach you the four rules above.

Challenge

Ask Claude how to make the door only open if the player has at least 5 coins from Mission 3.

7
Final project

Your own obby

AI idea · You're the creator

You've used AI for code, but the ideas, design, testing and fixing were yours. That's how real developers use AI. Today you plan and build a whole obstacle course (obby), then publish it for friends and family.

Step 1 · Design it on paper first

Sketch 5 stages. Mix what you've learned: lava, moving platforms, speed pads, coins, a secret door.

Step 2 · Checkpoints

Add a SpawnLocation (Model tab › Spawn) at the start of each stage. Put this script inside each one so players restart at the last checkpoint they touched:

local checkpoint = script.Parent
local Players = game:GetService("Players")

checkpoint.Touched:Connect(function(hit)
	local player = Players:GetPlayerFromCharacter(hit.Parent)
	if player then
		player.RespawnLocation = checkpoint
	end
end)
I'm building an obby in Roblox Studio with 5 stages. Here's my plan: [describe it]. What's one fun idea for stage 3 I haven't thought of? Give me the script and tell me where to put it.
Step 3 · Playtest

Have a family member play it while you watch. Don't help! Write down where they get stuck. Fix one thing at a time.

Step 4 · Publish

File › Publish to Roblox. Give it a name and description. With your grown-up, choose who can play it in the game's settings on create.roblox.com (start with private or friends only).

The golden rules
  1. Say what, where and how.
  2. One change at a time, then test.
  3. Always paste the error message.
  4. AI code is a first draft. You're the boss.
  5. Never share private info.
