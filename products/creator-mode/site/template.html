Questions parents ask

Does my kid need to know how to code?

No. A warm-up unit walks through installing Roblox Studio, finding the right panels and running a first script, with pictures for every click. If they can read and follow steps, they're ready. Best for ages 9–14.

What do we need?

A Windows PC or Mac (Roblox Studio doesn't run on phones, tablets or Chromebooks), the free Roblox Studio app, a Roblox account for your kid, and the Claude Desktop app on your Claude account. Studio's built-in connector links the two, and the warm-up shows you how to switch it on. Big builds go smoother on a paid Claude plan.

What exactly do I download?

A zip with Creator Quest (opens in any web browser: a warm-up, 8 big games and 21 skill quests, with profiles for each kid), the Creator Vault (118 Roblox model files), backup scripts for every big game, and a 6-page parent guide.

Isn't this just more screen time?

It's screen time where they make something. Skill quests take 10–15 minutes, so they fit a school night. The parent guide includes a Creator Hours chart so you can agree on a deal, like "an hour of building earns thirty minutes of playing."

Is the AI safe for kids?

Claude runs in the desktop app on your account, with you in the room, and asks before it changes anything in Studio. Mission 6 teaches the safety rules directly: never share personal info, check what AI says, and remember AI is a tool, not a friend.
